Invest Fest  ·  Young Builders  ·  Innovation

The 21-Year-Old Turning Plastic Into Fuel Took the Stage at Invest Fest

Julian Brown, 21, founder of NatureJAB, built a solar-powered microwave pyrolysis reactor in a backyard to turn plastic waste into a fuel he calls Plastoline, and this weekend his work was part of the conversation at Invest Fest 2026 in Atlanta. ANF keeps it honest: he did not invent pyrolysis, the claims deserve scrutiny, and he once landed in the hospital with burns. The lesson is not that he solved plastic. It is the posture: young, self-taught, and unwilling to wait for permission.

Invest Fest  ·  Wealth  ·  Community

40,000 People Went to Atlanta to Learn How to Own Something

Invest Fest 2026 took over the Georgia World Congress Center August 7 through 9, with organizers expecting more than 40,000 people across three days and names like Serena Williams, Steve Harvey, T.I., and Jermaine Dupri on the bill. Built by Earn Your Leisure, it turned a money podcast into the largest financial-literacy festival in the country. The lesson for Detroit: give young builders a room big enough and they will travel to be in it.

Invest Fest  ·  Founders  ·  Capital

Invest Fest Didn't Just Inspire Founders. It Funded Them.

A $125,000 tech pitch competition, $25,000 Operation HOPE business scholarships for five entrepreneurs, and a VIP deal room built to put founders across the table from investors. Motivation fills a room, but capital changes a year. With Black founders still landing a fraction of a percent of venture dollars, the first check is the hardest one to reach, and it is the exact wall ANF wants lowered in Atlanta and back home in Detroit.

Detroit  ·  Tech  ·  Manufacturing

A Detroit Startup Raised $11.5M to Build AI-Run Metal Factories

Nox Metals closed an $11.5 million seed round led by Hyperion, with Y Combinator and Palmer Luckey, to build vertically integrated, AI-run metal factories and reindustrialize from Detroit. It already supplies custom-cut metals to about 100 customers, from machine shops to Anduril and SpaceX, and is scaling its Detroit HQ instead of leaving for a coast.

Detroit  ·  Culture  ·  Music

Detroit Music Hall Is Building a $165 Million Home for the Next Century

Music Hall broke ground on a roughly $165 million, seven-story expansion beside its 1928 landmark: a 24,000 sq ft concert hall adding 1,900 seats, plus recording and practice studios, an academy, and a public welcome center, opening in 2028. For young Detroit artists, the studios and academy are the real story.

Black founders raised about 0.32% of all US venture dollars last year, and a record quarter changed little. So a wave of them is crossing the table, becoming investors to write the early, relationship-based checks they never got and fund the next generation themselves.

91.9% of creators now use at least one AI tool. But as AI makes output cheap and infinite, the scarce thing flips: a real human point of view, taste, and community become the premium, and the one thing a machine cannot mass-produce.

Detroit  ·  Small Business  ·  Food

A $25K Grant Turned a Family Name Into a Neighborhood Grocery

Karlyta Williams just opened The Counter Detroit, a small-format fresh grocery inside Shops on Six in the Bagley neighborhood, backed by a $25,000 Green Grocer grant. She named it after her great-grandparents' Southwest Detroit store and built it as a counter to the processed food on every shelf around it. The third Green Grocer ribbon cutting since the program relaunched in 2024, and proof of what a small grant does in the right hands.

Detroit  ·  Youth  ·  Founders

A Detroit Founder Is Paying Students to Start Companies

Detroit entrepreneur Ali Kain launched a $1,000 national scholarship for undergraduates preparing to build their own companies. There's no GPA requirement; it's decided by an essay about what you want to build. Most support shows up after the risk is gone. This one arrives early, while a student is still wondering if the idea is real. The amount is small. The message that someone believes in you is not.

Detroit  ·  Creative  ·  Youth

Detroit Put Its Young Artists in the Windows

Mint Artists Guild, a Palmer Park nonprofit founded in 2015, pays young Detroit creatives ages 14 to 21 and now shows their work in the windows of Black-owned and women-owned businesses across the city. It gives young artists a real public stage and ties the art to the small businesses next door. Not art behind an admission price downtown, but out where people live, free to see, impossible to miss.

Detroit  ·  Small Business

Detroit Voted, and Kraftologie Won $100,000

Trice Clark's Kraftologie, a creative learning lab, won the 2026 Comerica Hatch Detroit Contest, a $100,000 grand prize decided by more than 30,000 public votes, to open a space in Detroit's District 5. A grant committee decides in private. Here the city itself picked the winner, which means the space opening its doors is one Detroit actually asked for.

Youth  ·  Finance

Gen Z Is Buying Homes, and Doing It Alone

Gen Z hit a record 1 in 5 home purchase mortgage locks in Q2 2026, per ICE's Mortgage Monitor, and 53% of Gen Z buyers are purchasing on their own, more than double the millennial rate at the same age. Told ownership was out of reach, a majority are buying the house anyway, by themselves, with single women leading. Same instinct behind every builder we cover: ownership.
