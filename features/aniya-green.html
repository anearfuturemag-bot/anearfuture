A'Niya Green  ·  Photographer & Storyteller  ·  Detroit

A'Niya Green is 18 and already understands that a photograph can make someone feel seen. A Detroit photographer, she turned a first camera at 15 into a body of work with stores, artists, and athletes, and she is building a future where storytelling and care meet.

Who are you, and what are you building right now?

"My name is A'Niya Green, and I'm an 18 year old photographer from Detroit, Michigan, building a future centered around storytelling through photographs. Photography has never been just a hobby to me, it has been a way to learn people, connect with them on a deeper level, and create moments where they feel seen, valued, and heard. Through my work, I'm not just capturing images, I'm capturing emotions, experiences, and stories that people can carry with them."

What was the moment you decided to actually go for it?

"I decided to truly pursue photography when I stepped outside of my shy shell and challenged myself to get out of my comfort zone. In 2023, when I turned 15, I received my first camera, and from there I had to figure things out. I was simply a girl with a dream and a vision. Since then, I've had the opportunity to work with stores, artists, rappers, football players, art shows, and many other creatives. Every opportunity has taught me that photography is not just about taking pictures, it's about building relationships and creating something meaningful."

"When opportunity doesn't knock, you find a way to build the door."

What does Metro Detroit mean to your work?

"Detroit means everything to my work because this city represents resilience, creativity, and hustle. When people think of Detroit, they think about people who know how to make something out of nothing. One of Detroit's greatest strengths is our ability to build relationships. We live in a world that is obsessed with talent, but sometimes we overlook the power of growth, character, and determination. I believe that when opportunity doesn't knock, you find a way to build the door. Success and happiness are important, but I also believe character should be at the center of everything we do."

What's something people your age get wrong about what you do?

"A lot of people don't realize the mental capacity it takes to own and operate a business. It's not just about taking pictures, it's about working with people, understanding emotions, communicating effectively, and being able to meet the needs of others. Photography is more than an image. It's the memories behind the photo, the trust you build with people, and the connections created behind the lens. Another thing that keeps me grounded is my faith. Keeping God at the center of my journey is extremely important to me, because without Him I wouldn't be where I am today."

"It's the memories behind the photo, the trust you build, and the connections created behind the lens."

What's next? What does the near future look like for you?

"This fall, I'll be attending Michigan State University, where I'll be studying nursing while also continuing to develop my creativity and passion for storytelling. My goal is to understand people both inside and outside of the camera. Through nursing and creative direction, I want to combine my ability to connect with others and create work that makes people feel understood, represented, and inspired. The future I'm building is about more than photography, it's about using my gifts to impact people and tell stories that matter."
