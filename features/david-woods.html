Community & Leadership  ·  Detroit

Character
Over Talent.

David Woods  ·  Founder & CEO, Enjoy Detroit & SBOY Printing  ·  Detroit

David Woods has been building since 14, long enough to know the journey tests character more than talent. The founder and CEO of Enjoy Detroit and SBOY Printing & Promotions, an entrepreneur, community leader, and youth advocate, he opens The Blueprint with hard won wisdom on loneliness, patience, and trusting the process.

What do you wish someone had told you when you started?

"I wish somebody had told me that the journey will test your character more than your talent. I started entrepreneurship at 14 years old, thinking that if I worked hard enough, everything would eventually fall into place. But I learned that the real journey requires patience, discipline, sacrifice, faith, and trusting God's timing. Your gift might open the door, but your character is what allows you to stay in the room."

"Your gift might open the door, but your character is what allows you to stay in the room."

What's the part of building that nobody warns you about?

"Nobody really warns you how lonely building can become. Everybody can't go where God is taking you. Some people you start with will fall off, some will switch up, and sometimes you'll have to make the difficult decision to let people go because you've outgrown the relationship. Leadership taught me that you can love people, pray for people, and still understand that everybody isn't assigned to your entire journey."

What is this generation getting right, and where do you worry for them?

"This generation is fearless. They understand technology, creativity, entrepreneurship, and the power of building their own platform. They're not waiting for permission the way previous generations sometimes had to. My concern is that social media can make success look instant. I want our young people to understand that followers aren't the same as impact, attention isn't the same as purpose, and going viral isn't the same as building something that lasts. I want them to understand the power of patience, discipline, education, faith, and learning a real skill."

"Start with what you have. Serve people. Solve a problem. Let your work become your introduction."

What would you tell someone building in Detroit with no network and no cosign?

"I'd tell them: build anyway. I'm from Detroit, and I know what it feels like when nobody is coming to save you, introduce you, fund you, or cosign your vision. Start with what you have. Serve people. Solve a problem. Build relationships one person at a time, and let your work become your introduction. Don't be ashamed of starting small. Some of the greatest things God will build through you can start in places nobody is paying attention to. And never forget where you came from. Detroit teaches you resilience. If you can take the pressure, learn from your mistakes, keep your integrity, and continue showing up, your story can become somebody else's blueprint."

If you could pass down one principle that's kept you standing, what is it?

"Trust God and trust the process. I've experienced wins, losses, grief, disappointment, setbacks, and moments where I couldn't see how everything was going to work out. But God has continued to keep me standing. I've learned not to let what I'm going through right now make me forget where God is taking me. Success to me isn't just about money, recognition, or what I can build for myself anymore. It's about purpose. It's about how many people I can serve, how many young people I can pour into, how many leaders I can develop, and what I can leave behind when I'm gone. Never forget your story. Never forget where you came from. Keep God first, serve people, and trust the process."
