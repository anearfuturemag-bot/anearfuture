Detroit Is Paying to Keep Its 30-Year Businesses Alive

The Detroit Legacy Business Project grants up to 50,000 dollars to independently owned businesses that have served the city for 30 years or more.

A Near Future Editorial

July 2026

30 years

How long a business must have served Detroit to qualify

Detroit is putting money behind the businesses that never left. The Detroit Legacy Business Project awards grants to independently owned shops that have operated in the city for 30 years or more and shaped its culture along the way, from corner staples to the places that outlasted every downturn.

The grants are real. The program runs microgrants of 2,500 and 5,000 dollars and larger grants of 15,000 and 50,000 dollars, delivered as prepayments or reimbursements for eligible projects. Recipients are chosen on a scored application plus a site visit, so the money follows businesses that are genuinely rooted, not just paperwork.

"A city that funds its 30-year businesses is a city protecting its own memory."

There is a lesson here for young builders too. Detroit is publicly rewarding longevity, the founders who stayed on the same block for three decades and became part of the neighborhood's identity. In an economy that worships the quick exit, a city writing checks for endurance is saying the quiet part out loud: staying is a win.

It is also a preservation move. When a legacy business closes, a piece of the culture that made a neighborhood worth moving to goes with it. Keeping these doors open protects the character that every new founder and creative is actually drawn to.

How to Apply

  • AwardMicrogrants of $2,500 and $5,000, plus grants of $15,000 and $50,000
  • Who qualifiesIndependently owned businesses with a physical, public location in Detroit, open to the public for 30 years or more, that have impacted the city’s culture
  • How chosenA scored application plus a site visit and supporting documents
  • How to applyApply through the official City of Detroit program page below. Check the current round’s deadline before submitting.

This is the unglamorous work that keeps a city worth building in. The businesses that stayed are the ones that made the neighborhoods matter. Detroit paying to keep them open is the city investing in its own foundation.

Longevity is a flex. Detroit is finally funding it.
