He Built It in His Bedroom. At 20, Hollywood Let Him Direct It.

Kane Parsons made the viral Backrooms series as a teen. Now 20, he directed the A24 and Atomic Monster feature adaptation, which has grossed around $50M.

A Near Future Editorial

July 2026

$50M

What the feature has grossed, directed by the kid who made the original in his bedroom.

Kane Parsons was a teenager making horror videos in his bedroom when he created The Backrooms, a viral YouTube series about an endless, uncanny maze of empty rooms. It racked up hundreds of millions of views and turned a self-taught kid into one of the internet's most watched young creators. Then Hollywood came knocking.

Now he is 20, and he directed the feature film. A24 and James Wan's Atomic Monster backed a movie version of The Backrooms and, unusually, put Parsons himself in the director's chair rather than handing his idea to a veteran. The film has pulled in around 50 million dollars worldwide.

That is a rare leap. Studios almost never let the young creator of a viral idea direct the big-budget version of it. Parsons earned it by proving, video after video, that he could build a whole eerie world with almost nothing, and by owning the thing he made instead of selling it off.

"He built it in his bedroom. Then Hollywood let him direct it himself."

The lesson is ownership. Parsons did not pitch an idea and hope someone hired him. He built the thing, grew the audience, and made himself the only person who could direct the adaptation. Do the work in public long enough, and you become undeniable.

This is the builder path ANF keeps spotlighting. Make the thing yourself, build the audience, and own it, until you are the only choice to lead it.

He made it in his bedroom. Now he is directing it for the world.
