Rejected by 15 Colleges, He Just Raised $10 Million at 19.

Zach Yadegari, 19, co-founded Cal AI, saw it acquired, and raised a $10M seed for Persona, an AI assistant and wearable.

A Near Future Editorial

October 2026

$10M

The seed round he raised at 19 for Persona, after 15 colleges turned him down.

Zach Yadegari started coding at seven, growing up on Long Island, and built and sold his first business by 16 for a reported 100,000 dollars. Then came the rejection that went viral: with a 4.0 GPA, he applied to 18 universities and 15 of them, including Harvard, Yale, and Stanford, told him no.

He did not slow down to argue about it. Yadegari co-founded Cal AI, a calorie-tracking app, in 2024, and it rocketed past older competitors to more than 30 million dollars in annual revenue before MyFitnessPal acquired it in early 2026. He left that summer to start over.

Now he is building again. In October 2026, Yadegari raised a 10 million dollar seed round, led by Vine Ventures, for Persona, a personal AI assistant paired with a 179 dollar wearable band you trigger with a button or a flick of the wrist, with a free beta already running inside iMessage.

"Fifteen colleges said no. At 19 he had already built, sold, and started again."

The rejections could have been the story. Yadegari made them a footnote by letting the work answer instead, building a real company while the admissions letters piled up. It is the clearest proof that the gatekeepers you are taught to chase are not the only way the door opens.

This is the near-future founder ANF keeps spotlighting. Do not wait for an institution to validate you. Build the thing, and let the results make the case.

The colleges passed. The market did not.
