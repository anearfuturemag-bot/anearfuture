65% of Gen Z Say They Are Creators Now

A 2026 report finds 65% of Gen Z identify as creators, 56% find creator content more relevant than TV or film, and 41% use social as their search engine.

A Near Future Editorial

July 2026

65%

Of Gen Z now identify as creators, not just viewers

The line between audience and creator just collapsed. A 2026 report from the Influencer Marketing Factory found that 65 percent of Gen Z now identify as creators, not just people who watch, but people who make and think about building a personal brand.

The rest of the numbers explain why. 56 percent of Gen Z say creator content is more relevant to them than TV or film, and 41 percent now use social platforms as their primary search engine. For this generation, the creator economy is not a side channel. It is the culture, the newsfeed, and the search bar all at once.

"For Gen Z, making content is not a hobby. It is the default way to exist online."

This flips the old advice on its head. For years, being a creator was treated as a risky thing you did instead of a real career. Now it is the baseline expectation, and the real question is not whether to create but what to actually build with the skill everyone your age already has.

That is where most people stall. Identifying as a creator is easy and nearly universal now. Turning that identity into a body of work, a real audience, and eventually a business is the part that separates the 65 percent who claim the title from the few who make a living from it.

This is the honest challenge ANF keeps putting in front of people. Everyone your age is a creator now. That means the title alone means nothing. The work, the point of view, and the community you build on top of it are the whole game.

Being a creator is the starting line. Almost no one treats it like one.
