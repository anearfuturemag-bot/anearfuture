The Rent Is Why Most Brands Never Open. 313 Cinnamon Rolls Is Changing That.

Brandy Jackson turned her Midtown vegan bakery into a launchpad for other founders. Her new Weekend Collection puts local makers in the shop, and this fall The Residency will hand emerging entrepreneurs a storefront without a lease.

A Near Future Editorial

August 2026

Ask most people why they never opened the business they talk about, and the answer is almost never the idea. The idea is fine. The recipe works, the product sells at the pop-up, the following is real. What stops them is the lease: first month, last month, deposit, a year of rent signed before a single customer walks in. The gap between a good concept and an open door is money most first-time founders do not have. So the door stays closed.

In Midtown Detroit, Brandy Jackson is trying to move that door.

Jackson is the founder of 313 Cinnamon Rolls, a woman-owned vegan bakery she describes as a "Cinntique" on Garfield Street. The shop just passed its first anniversary in Midtown, and instead of using that milestone to guard what she built, she is opening it up to other people trying to build their own thing.

On Saturday, August 22, Jackson launched The Weekend Collection, a marketplace that runs inside the bakery every Saturday and Sunday. It brings a curated mix of small businesses, makers, and creatives into the space, giving shoppers another way to buy local and, just as important, giving those businesses a room full of customers they did not have to find on their own. Rather than run as a crowded open vendor market, The Weekend Collection features a small, selected group of businesses plus an invited guest business each weekend. Because 313 is proudly vegan, every food and beverage vendor in the mix is 100% vegan too.

"313 Cinnamon Rolls is a place where community, creativity, and opportunity come together. Being active and available in our community will always be our number one priority."

The marketplace, though, is the preview. The bigger move comes this fall, when Jackson plans to launch The Residency at 313 Cinnamon Rolls. The Residency is built to attack the exact problem that keeps so many founders stuck: it gives emerging entrepreneurs an affordable, temporary storefront environment where they can test a concept, meet real customers, and build a business without the cost of holding a permanent retail lease. It is a real address, on a real street, without the year-long commitment that usually gates entry.

That is a different kind of generosity than a discount or a shoutout. It is infrastructure. A founder who gets a few weeks behind an actual counter learns things a spreadsheet cannot teach: what sells, what the foot traffic is really like, how to price, how to talk to a customer who is deciding in real time. That education normally costs a lease to get. Jackson is trying to make it cost a lot less.

It matters that this is coming from a founder and not a fund. Jackson is not a landlord with extra square footage or a program with a grant cycle. She is a small-business owner one year into her own storefront, choosing to turn the thing she fought for into a door other people can walk through. The community that formed around 313, by her own account, is what made her want to build The Residency in the first place. She is paying forward the exact access she needed.

Here is the ANF Take. The rent is the filter. It quietly decides which Detroit brands get to exist and which ones stay side hustles forever, and it filters hardest on the founders with the least cushion. What Jackson is building treats access as the product, not the byproduct. A weekend market plus a lease-free residency is a small operation on paper, but the model is the point: the founders who actually change a neighborhood are the ones who leave the door open behind them. Detroit does not have a talent problem. It has an on-ramp problem. 313 Cinnamon Rolls just built one.

If you have a concept and the only thing standing between you and a storefront is the money to hold one, watch this space. 313 Cinnamon Rolls is at 119 Garfield St., Suite 200, in Detroit. Details on The Weekend Collection and The Residency are at 313CinnamonRolls.com and on Instagram at @313CinnamonRolls. The next brand that opens in this city might open in that room first.
