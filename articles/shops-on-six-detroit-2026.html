Detroit Just Gave Its Makers Real Shelves

Shops on Six, a new marketplace on Detroit's west side, opened to give local entrepreneurs their first brick-and-mortar space.

A Near Future Editorial

July 2026

Six Mile

The McNichols corridor where Shops on Six put Detroit makers on real shelves.

Detroit just handed its small makers something most of them have never had: real shelves. Shops on Six, a new marketplace on the city's west side near McNichols and San Juan, opened this month to give local entrepreneurs a brick-and-mortar home and the neighborhood better access to fresh goods.

For the vendors, it is a first. Sydni Warner, founder of The Duey Dealer, is among those making her brick-and-mortar debut, saying she is just excited to finally have products on shelves. Karlis Pulley of Sweet Aroma Candle Company is opening a physical spot there too. For both, it is the jump from selling online or at pop-ups to having an actual place customers can walk into.

"A shelf is a small thing until it is the first one you have ever had."

That jump is the hard one. Plenty of Detroiters can make a product, build a following, and sell out of a car trunk or an Instagram DM. Getting from there to a real storefront, with foot traffic and a permanent address, is the wall most makers hit. A shared marketplace lowers that wall for everyone under one roof.

It does two things at once. It gives founders a real retail shot without the cost of a solo lease, and it gives the neighborhood a reason to shop its own blocks. That loop, local makers and local dollars feeding each other, is how a commercial strip comes back to life.

This is the unglamorous groundwork ANF loves to see. Not a headline raise, a shelf. The first real shelf for a founder is where a hobby quietly turns into a business.

Detroit gave its makers a place to be found. Now go find them.
