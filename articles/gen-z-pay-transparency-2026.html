Gen Z Is Breaking the Oldest Rule at Work: Don't Talk About Pay

Nearly 40% of Gen Z openly discuss their salaries, almost double Gen X, and 81% believe transparency makes pay fairer.

A Near Future Editorial

July 2026

81%

Of Gen Z believe pay transparency improves pay equity

For generations, the rule at work was simple: do not talk about what you make. Gen Z is throwing it out. Nearly 40 percent of Gen Z workers openly discuss their salaries, almost double the rate of Gen X at 22 percent, and 81 percent believe pay transparency improves pay equity, compared with just 28 percent of baby boomers.

This is not oversharing. It is strategy. When 44 percent of Gen Z rank pay transparency and fairness among their top job factors, ahead of many traditional perks, they are treating salary information as leverage. You cannot negotiate against a number you are not allowed to know, and this generation refuses to stay in the dark.

"The old rule kept workers quiet and underpaid. Gen Z figured out who that rule protected."

It fits a bigger pattern. Gen Z job-hoppers who leave for better pay end up earning 15 to 20 percent more than those who stay, and most cite growth and compensation as the reason. Combine open salary talk with a willingness to walk, and you get a generation that has quietly rebalanced the power between worker and employer.

There is real pressure underneath it. Nearly half of Gen Z did not feel financially secure heading into 2026, so the stakes of getting paid fairly are not abstract. Transparency is how they are trying to fix a system that told them to work hard and stay quiet.

This is the same ownership instinct ANF keeps covering. Own your worth, know your number, and do not let an old rule set it for you. Gen Z is applying founder logic to a paycheck: information is power, and silence is expensive.

The taboo protected the people signing the checks. Gen Z stopped playing along.
