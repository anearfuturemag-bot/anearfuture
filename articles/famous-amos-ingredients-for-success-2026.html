2,800 Founders Applied. Three Got the Check.

Famous Amos handed three Black owned businesses $50,000 each through its Ingredients for Success program, and the applicant pool doubled to a record 2,800 in a single year. The capital is real. The line is getting longer. Here is how to get in it.

A Near Future Editorial

August 2026

$150K

Awarded to three Black founders this year

The numbers move fast, so start with the ones that matter. Famous Amos, through its Ingredients for Success program run with the U.S. Black Chambers, just awarded 150,000 dollars in capital, 50,000 dollars each to three early stage Black owned businesses. Alongside the money comes mentorship, networking, and educational resources built to keep a young company alive past the first hard year. The winners were announced July 29, and the program is now in its sixth year.

The part that should get your attention is not the check. It is the crowd. This year the program drew 2,800 applications, its largest pool ever and more than double the number from 2025. That is thousands of Black founders raising a hand for the same 50,000 dollars, and it is the clearest signal yet of how many people are building right now with no easy path to a first check.

"The applicant pool did not double because the money got bigger. It doubled because the room got more crowded."

Look at who won and you see the range. The Brunch Munch, a St. Petersburg food truck founded by Ashley Reed, uses food as a way to hold community and cultural spaces together. Maison Black, founded by Tori Nichel in New York, is a fashion platform that puts Black designers in front of buyers and hands them market visibility they rarely get. The Marriage Pinnacle, out of Maryland, is a relationship support platform co-founded by a licensed clinical psychologist and a relationship coach, built to help Black couples strengthen their families. A food truck, a fashion marketplace, and a mental health platform. Three completely different businesses, one thing in common: someone filled out the form.

This is the piece we keep coming back to at ANF. Black founders in the United States raised roughly 643 million dollars in the first quarter of 2026, the strongest quarter in nearly four years, and it still amounted to a fraction of a percent of all venture dollars. The talent was never the gap. Access to the first check is. Programs like Ingredients for Success are one of the few doors built to close that gap on purpose, which is exactly why 2,800 people knocked.

Since 2020, the program has put 900,000 dollars behind 18 businesses. That is not venture-scale money and no one pretends it is. What it is, is early money with no equity taken and a network attached, arriving at the stage where most businesses quietly die. For a founder with a real product and no rich relatives, 50,000 dollars and a mentor is the difference between a good idea and a going concern.

So here is the honest read for anyone building in Detroit and beyond. The crowd doubling is not a reason to count yourself out. It is proof the check is real and the demand is real, and the people who win these are almost never the most connected. They are the ones who treated the application like it was worth their afternoon. Most people saw 2,800 applicants and told themselves it was a long shot. Three people saw the same number and applied anyway.

How to Get in Line for the Next Round

Ingredients for Success runs on an annual cycle, so the move now is to be ready before the next application window opens rather than scrambling when it does.

Where to apply and watch for dates: the program lives at FamousAmosIngredientsforSuccess.com. Bookmark it and check back, since new-cycle applications and deadlines are posted there first.

Who it is for: early stage Black business owners with a real product or service and a business that is up and running. Past winners span food, fashion, tech, and services, so the category matters less than traction and clarity.

Stay in the loop: the program is run in partnership with the U.S. Black Chambers, Inc. at usblackchambers.org, and Famous Amos posts updates on its Instagram. Follow both so the next open window does not pass you by.

How to prepare now: get your numbers in order, write a clear one paragraph story of what you build and who it serves, and gather proof of traction such as sales, customers, or a working product. The founders who win tend to be the ones who could apply the day the form goes live.

The capital exists. It is not a rumor and it is not reserved for people with connections you do not have. The only thing separating the founders who get it from the ones who talk about getting it is whether they filled out the form. When the next window opens, be in the 2,800. Then be in the three.
