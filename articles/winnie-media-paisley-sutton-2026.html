Detroit Keeps Exporting Its Storytellers. Paisley Sutton Came Home.

The Detroit-born director stacked credits in Los Angeles, then moved back to build Winnie Media with her mother, a production house built to put marginalized voices on camera.

A Near Future Editorial

August 2026

The usual Detroit talent story ends at the city limits. A kid grows up here, gets good, and takes the gift somewhere with a bigger name on the door. Paisley Sutton ran that play, and then she ran it in reverse.

Sutton is a Detroit-born director and producer who built her early career in Los Angeles. Her credits include co-writing Anderson Paak's Grammy-nominated video, directing the short documentary Descendants, and producing digital campaigns for brands like Prime Video and National Geographic. It is the kind of resume most people leave home to chase and never bring back.

She brought it back. Five years ago she moved home to Detroit, and in 2021 she started Winnie Media with her mother, Patrice Cheesman. The company is named after Winnifred Cheesman, the great-grandmother who immigrated to Detroit from Barbados in the 1920s. The mission is direct: make storytelling accessible to marginalized voices, especially in Detroit.

"A smooth sea never made a skilled sailor."

It was not a clean launch. For years the business took a back seat while Sutton worked as a creative producer for an LA-based agency, gaining skills but deferring the dream. A documentary project her mother found in 2024 became the catalyst to refocus. Winnie Media is a full-time operation now, with a team of contractors and collaborators, and by Sutton's own account it is only this year starting to make a splash in the Detroit creative scene.

The work has a point of view. Winnie Media specializes in music videos, documentaries, social campaigns, and photography rooted in cultural preservation, surrealism, and Black pride. In February 2026 the company began creating for the Middle Passage Ceremonies and Port Markers Project, a non-profit commemorating more than 90 U.S. arrival sites of captive Africans during the trans-Atlantic slave trade. Its first original film is in research and development, with production planned for spring 2027.

Here is the ANF Take. The talent was never the problem, and it never left. Detroit has always produced storytellers. What it has not always had is people who build the studio here instead of sending the work somewhere else. Sutton is betting that the infrastructure, the contracts, the crews, the client base, can live in Detroit and serve Detroit at the same time. That bet is how a city keeps its own culture instead of licensing it out.

A production company named after a woman who crossed an ocean to get to Detroit is not an accident. It is the whole thesis. You do not have to leave to build something worth seeing. Sometimes coming home is the move.
