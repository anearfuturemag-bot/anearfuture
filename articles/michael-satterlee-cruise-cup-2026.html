One Video Got 50 Million Views. His Real Skill Came Before That.

Michael Satterlee, 18, taught himself 3D printing in high school and built a first business, then turned a viral can-cooler video into Cruise Cup, doing $300K a month.

A Near Future Editorial

July 2026

$300K

A single month of sales, built on years of learning to make and sell products first.

Michael Satterlee is eighteen, still living in his childhood home outside Albany, New York, and he has quietly been building for years. In high school, he taught himself to use 3D printers and started Solefully, a shoe accessories brand, learning how to make a product, sell it online, and turn a machine in his bedroom into revenue.

That first business funded the big one. Using the same printers, Satterlee designed a can cooler with a twist, one that opens on both the top and bottom so a can slides right in, and posted a low-budget video of himself using it on a Dr Pepper. It hit 50 million views. Instead of letting the moment pass, he already had a product ready, and turned the attention into sales.

The numbers are real. His company, Cruise Cup, and its viral tactical reload can holder pulled in about 300,000 dollars in a single month, verified by Business Insider from his Shopify dashboard. He is now foregoing college to scale it full-time.

"Attention is cheap. Turning it into a business you own is the rare skill."

But the story is not go viral. Millions of people go viral and end up with nothing but a spike and a memory. Satterlee spent years learning to make and sell products first, so when 50 million people showed up, he had something to sell them, and a second brand behind it. That is the difference between a moment and a machine.

This is the modern playbook ANF keeps pointing at. Do not chase virality. Build the skill and the product first, so you are ready when the eyes show up.

One video got the views. Everything he built before it is why they turned into a business.
