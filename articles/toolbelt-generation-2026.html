Gen Z Is Picking Up the Toolbelt

60% of Gen Z plan to pursue the skilled trades. As AI shakes the desk job, this generation is betting on work that can't be automated and can't be outsourced.

A Near Future Editorial

July 2026

60%

Of Gen Z plan to pursue the skilled trades in 2026

A generation raised on screens is reaching for a toolbelt. New surveys put it plainly: 60% of Gen Z plan to pursue the skilled trades, and more than half say they are seriously considering blue-collar work. Enrollment in two-year vocational programs has jumped double digits since 2020.

The reasons are practical, not nostalgic. College tuition kept climbing while the promise attached to a degree got shakier. At the same time, AI started eating into the exact white-collar desk jobs those degrees were supposed to guarantee. A trade is harder to automate and impossible to offshore. You cannot outsource a wire that needs pulling in a Detroit basement.

"You can't automate a trade. The toolbelt is the one job an algorithm can't take."

There's a mindset shift underneath the numbers. For a long time the trades were framed as the backup plan, the thing you did if college didn't work out. Gen Z is flipping that. They see a licensed electrician who owns their schedule and their rate as more free, not less, than a salaried worker waiting to learn if the next round of layoffs has their name on it.

It also lines up with something this generation already believes: ownership beats a title. A tradesperson can become a contractor, then a business, then an employer. The path from apprentice to owner is short and real, and it doesn't require permission from a gatekeeper.

For the builders ANF covers, this is familiar territory. The instinct to make something with your hands, to own the work instead of renting your time, is the same instinct behind every young founder we cover. The toolbelt generation and the founder generation are the same people, choosing to build.

The trades were never the fallback. For a lot of Gen Z, they are the plan.
