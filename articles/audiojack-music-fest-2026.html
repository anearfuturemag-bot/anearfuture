The Talent Is Here. The Stage Usually Isn't. AudioJack Fixes That.

We Are Culture Creators built a festival to put Detroit's underground under one roof. JMSN headlines more than 50 artists across four stages at Lincoln Factory on August 29.

A Near Future Editorial

August 2026

Detroit has never had a talent problem. The city presses out singers, rappers, producers, DJs, and poets the way it once pressed out cars. What it has too often lacked is the room to hold all of that at once, a stage big enough that the scene does not have to leave town to be seen. Too many Detroit artists build their following one city at a time, everywhere except home.

On Saturday, August 29, a new festival tries to change that math.

AudioJack Music Fest takes over Lincoln Factory at 1331 Holden St. from 3 p.m. to 4 a.m., a thirteen-hour run with more than 50 artists across four curated stages. The bill mixes live bands, hip-hop, R&B, DJs, spoken word, and visual art into a single night, and it is built to feel less like a concert and more like a snapshot of the entire underground standing in one place.

Headlining is JMSN, born Christian Berishaj, the Eastpointe native who has spent a decade proving an independent artist can shapeshift on his own terms. He passed through Atlantic and Universal Motown out of high school, walked away from both, and built a catalog on the indie route that pulled comparisons to Frank Ocean and the Weeknd and collaborations with Kendrick Lamar and Ab-Soul. His 2023 album "Soft Spot" went viral and widened his audience, and rather than repeat it, he pivoted hard into raw guitar-driven rock on his latest, "...it's only about u if you think it is." He is the right face for a festival like this precisely because he refused to let anybody else set his direction.

"That's all you want, is to get eyes on what you're doing. The whole idea of pushing a record is to get it seen and heard."

The name under the whole thing matters more than the headliner, though. AudioJack is produced by We Are Culture Creators, a Detroit nonprofit built to open doors for artists and creatives through cultural programming, education, entrepreneurship, and community-driven events. The festival is the visible tip of that mission: a real stage, a real crowd, and a real payday for a lineup that goes well past the headliner, including Detroit rapper GT, Nasaan, Taylor Bentley, and rising R&B vocalist Nylon Keies, plus dozens of emerging artists, DJs, and poets sharing the same four stages.

That is the part worth sitting with. A festival is not just a party. For an emerging artist, a slot on a four-stage bill next to a national headliner is a credential, a tape, a room full of new listeners, and proof that somebody in your own city bet on you before the algorithm did. Multiply that across 50-plus artists in one night and you are not throwing an event, you are building infrastructure.

Here is the ANF Take. Scenes do not die from a lack of talent. They die from a lack of stages. When the only path to being seen runs through Los Angeles, Atlanta, or New York, a city becomes a farm team, developing artists for somebody else's roster. What We Are Culture Creators is doing with AudioJack is the opposite move: keep the wave home, put the whole ecosystem in one building for a night, and let Detroit be the place the story starts instead of the place the artist left. Do that a few years in a row and it stops being a festival and starts being a pipeline.

AudioJack Music Fest runs Saturday, August 29, from 3 p.m. to 4 a.m. at Lincoln Factory, 1331 Holden St., Detroit. Tickets are on sale through Eventbrite, with lineup updates from We Are Culture Creators on Instagram at @weareculturecreators. If you have ever watched a Detroit artist get famous somewhere else, this is the night the city keeps one for itself.
