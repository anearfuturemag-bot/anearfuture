A Metro Detroit Startup Just Raised $13.1 Million to Scale Its Factory

NxLite, a Canton clean-tech company, closed a $13.1 million Series A to grow production of its energy-saving window coatings, and it is building that capacity right here.

A Near Future Editorial

July 2026

$13.1M

Series A close for NxLite, a Canton, Michigan clean-tech maker

A Metro Detroit clean-tech company just told the capital markets it can build the hard stuff at home. NxLite, based in Canton, closed a $13.1 million Series A financing and added a $3.5 million debt facility from RSF Social Finance, capital it will use to scale up manufacturing of its energy-saving coatings for glass and windows.

The raise beat the plan. NxLite had targeted $10 million, then blew past it, closing at $13.1 million after a first close of $9.2 million back in October 2025 and a second closing that pulled in both new and returning investors. The equity round was led by Crabtree Lane Alt, with new backing from VoLo Earth Ventures and continued support from earlier investors including Earth Foundry, MUUS, New Climate Ventures, and ACT Venture Partners.

"The region's story is not only culture and comeback. It is deep tech, real capital, and factory jobs that stay."

The product is unglamorous and that is the point. NxLite makes air-stable, low-emissivity coatings that cut how much heat passes through glass and polymer surfaces, a lighter alternative to conventional products. It is the kind of quiet infrastructure that never trends, but it lowers energy bills in real buildings and it takes real factories to produce.

That factory is local. Since the first close, NxLite has expanded its Advanced Innovation and Manufacturing Center in Canton, the site where the company is turning lab results into production. The debt facility matters here too: it is non-dilutive capital aimed specifically at manufacturing scale-up, which means the founders can build capacity without giving away more of the company to do it.

The money also bought experience. NxLite added Erin Sawyer to its board, a supply chain executive who ran sourcing for electric powertrains at Wrightspeed and earlier helped scale global supply chains at Tesla for the Model S and Model X. Bringing that kind of operator onto the board is a signal that the company is thinking about volume, not just validation.

For everyone building in and around Detroit, the takeaway is bigger than one company's cap table. A regional startup raising eight figures to grow a factory here, rather than moving production somewhere cheaper, is proof that the advanced-manufacturing path is open and that ambitious builders do not have to leave to be taken seriously. Keep the hard work at home, and the reasons to stay start to stack up.
