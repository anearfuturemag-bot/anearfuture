A Diagnosis at 24 Ended One Dream. She Built a Better One Out of It.

After a muscular dystrophy diagnosis at 24, Keisha Greaves founded Girls Chronically Rock, an adaptive and empowering clothing line for the disability community.

A Near Future Editorial

September 2026

24

Her age when a muscular dystrophy diagnosis forced her to rethink the whole plan.

Keisha Greaves was 24, in graduate school for her MBA, and moving toward a career in fashion when her body started to change. She began having trouble moving her limbs and was diagnosed with limb-girdle muscular dystrophy. For a while she stayed in denial, unsure what her future held.

The fashion dream did not die. It changed shape. As Greaves adjusted to living with a disability, she noticed how few stylish, adaptive clothing options existed for people like her. So the Cambridge, Massachusetts native, who holds a degree in fashion design and merchandising along with her MBA, founded Girls Chronically Rock in 2017 to make them.

The brand grew into a platform. Girls Chronically Rock sells inspirational and adaptive apparel, including adaptive swimsuits, designed for the chronic illness and disability community, and Greaves has taken the message to Good Morning America, the Today show, and ABC News, building confidence into every piece.

"The diagnosis closed one door. She designed a whole new one and held it open for others."

It would have been understandable to treat the diagnosis as the end of the plan. Greaves treated it as the brief. She built the clothing she could not find and turned her own hardest moment into confidence for a community the fashion industry kept ignoring. That is building from exactly where you stand.

This is the resilience ANF exists to spotlight. When life rewrites your plan, build something the version of you before the diagnosis never could have.

She lost the old dream. She built a better one, and made room for everyone like her.
