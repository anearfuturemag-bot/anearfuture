A Detroit Couple Just Made History on Livernois. Doors Open Saturday.

John and Allyson Smith open Detroit's first Black-owned Beyond Juicery + Eatery in the University District on November 21, with jobs for young adults and college students.

A Near Future Editorial

November 2026

1st

The brand's first franchise owned by a Black husband-and-wife team, out of more than 55 locations. John Smith built his name in Detroit one workout at a time. He founded Grindtime Fitness, a Detroit gym that has served thousands of residents through exercise, accountability and community, and with his wife Allyson he has backed youth wellness programs focused on fitness and nutrition. For years the message to the neighborhood was the same: take care of yourself. The next question was obvious. Where do you eat after the workout?

So they built the answer. On Saturday, November 21, the Smiths open Beyond Juicery + Eatery University District at 17149 Livernois Avenue, near the University of Detroit Mercy. It is Detroit's first Black-owned Beyond Juicery, and the Michigan brand's first franchise owned by a Black husband-and-wife team. Beyond Juicery started in Birmingham in 2005, began franchising in 2018 and now runs more than 55 locations.

The details are what make it a neighborhood business and not just a new storefront. The restaurant will hire young adults and college students, giving them real experience in hospitality, operations and customer service. It plans to join Michigan's Restaurant Meal Program, so eligible SNAP recipients could use a Bridge Card for prepared meals, pending program approval. Mayor Mary Sheffield is set to join the ribbon cutting on November 20, and the doors open to the public at 11 a.m. the next morning, with free tote bags for the first 200 customers who make a purchase.

"Representation in ownership matters, but so does the impact we create through it."

That line from Allyson Smith is the whole point. A franchise is a system anyone can buy into. What the Smiths are adding is the part you cannot buy: jobs for the young people on their block, fresh food where it has been hard to find, and a front-row look at what ownership actually takes. The kid working the register this winter is watching two Detroiters run a business up close.

This is the kind of builder ANF exists to spotlight. Build where you live, hire the people next to you, and let them see how it is done.

They spent years getting Detroit moving. Now they are feeding it too. SOURCES: EIN Presswire
