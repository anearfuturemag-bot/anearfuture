A Detroit Kid Who Snuck Into Clubs Now Has 200 Million Streams

Detroit-born producer Topher Jones went from sneaking into dance clubs as a teenager to 200 million-plus Spotify streams and stages shared with Diplo and Tiesto.

A Near Future Editorial

July 2026

200M+

Spotify streams for the Detroit-born producer Topher Jones

Topher Jones started where a lot of Detroit music does, sneaking into dance clubs as a teenager to be near the sound he loved. The Detroit-born producer, known as King Topher, has since crossed 200 million Spotify streams and shared stages with Diplo, Tiesto, John Summit, and Kaskade, and played Tomorrowland along the way.

He is not slowing down in 2026. His Rave Jesus project, a faith-driven turn on the dance music that built his name, keeps gaining momentum and has pulled multiple 2026 Electronic Dance Music Award nominations, including for the collaboration Devil is a Liar with AndyG.

"Detroit does not just make the sound. It raises the people who take it worldwide."

The arc is the Detroit story in a different genre. A kid with no clear door in, obsessed enough to find his way inside anyway, builds a craft quietly for years, then turns up on the biggest stages in the world without ever losing where he is from. Same blueprint as the founders we cover, different medium.

His second act matters as much as his numbers. Reinventing an established sound into something personal, the Rave Jesus turn, is the risky move most artists avoid once they are winning. Betting on a new lane while you are already established is exactly how you stay relevant instead of getting stuck.

This is the range of what ANF means by builder. Not only founders and grants. A kid from Detroit who built a global sound from a club he was not old enough to enter is building something too.

Detroit raised the sound. The world is streaming it now.
