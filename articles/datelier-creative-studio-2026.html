Everybody Wants the Shot. D'ATELIER Owns the Room.

Oso Unique and Jon Jamar built a Black-owned studio to hold hair, beauty, fashion, and photography under one roof. Now they are moving into a bigger Detroit-area space.

A Near Future Editorial

August 2026

Most creative work in this city happens on borrowed time. A photographer books an hour in somebody else's studio. A stylist works out of a rented chair. A brand shoot gets squeezed into whatever room is free before the next person's slot starts. The talent is here. The space almost never belongs to the people making the work.

D'ATELIER Creative Studio was built to break that pattern, and this month it is getting bigger.

Oso Unique and Jon Jamar started D'ATELIER as one roof for the disciplines that usually get scattered across the city: hair, beauty, fashion, and photography. Their own backgrounds span all four. Rather than pick a lane, they built a place where the lanes meet. The Black-owned studio functions as a sanctuary for photoshoots, content creation, events, and the kind of collaboration that only happens when creatives are in the same room instead of trading files.

"This next chapter is about creating more, collaborating more, and building something special for Detroit's creative community."

That next chapter is a move. D'ATELIER previously operated out of a space at 13380 Capital Street in Oak Park. Now the team is transforming a new, larger Detroit-area location, a former hookah bar currently under construction and remodel, into the studio's next home. The exact address has not been released yet, but the intent is clear from what the founders are saying: more room to create, more room to gather, more room for other people's work, not just their own.

The detail that matters is the word sanctuary. A studio is not just square footage. It is who gets to walk in and feel like the place was built with them in mind. For a lot of Detroit creatives, especially Black creatives working across beauty, fashion, and image-making, that feeling has been rare. You can rent access to a room. It is much harder to find one that treats your work like it belongs there.

Here is the ANF Take. Owning the room changes everything. When you rent time by the hour, the space sets the terms and the clock runs the shoot. When you own it, you set the culture. D'ATELIER is not just leveling up its own operation, it is building infrastructure the next wave of Detroit creatives can plug into. That is the difference between a scene that exports its talent and a scene that keeps it. The studios have to live here for the culture to stay here.

A bigger room is a small headline until you notice what it really is: two Detroit creatives deciding the work they care about deserves a permanent address in this city. If you have ever made your best work in somebody else's space, you already understand why that matters.
