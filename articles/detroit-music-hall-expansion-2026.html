Detroit Music Hall Is Building a $165 Million Home for the Next Century

Music Hall broke ground on a roughly 165 million dollar, seven-story expansion beside its 1928 landmark, with a new concert hall, studios, and academy space.

A Near Future Editorial

July 2026

$165M

Expansion Music Hall is building beside its 1928 landmark

Detroit's Music Hall is building its future. The Music Hall Center for the Performing Arts broke ground this summer on a roughly 165 million dollar, seven-story expansion next to its historic 1928 building at Brush and Madison downtown, with an opening targeted for 2028.

The addition is not small. At its core is a 24,000 square foot concert hall that adds about 1,900 seats, alongside a smaller recital hall, recording and practice studios, leasable space for music industry professionals, and a public welcome center. It is a full ecosystem for making and staging music, not just a bigger room.

"A city does not build a 165 million dollar home for music unless it believes in the people who will fill it."

For young Detroit artists, the studios and academy space are the real story. A place to learn, record, and perform under one roof lowers the barrier between having talent and having a career. That is the gap that swallows most young creatives, and infrastructure like this is how a city starts to close it.

It is also a bet on permanence. Festivals come and go, but a building is a promise. Detroit is saying its music scene deserves a landmark built for the next hundred years, right next to the one that has stood since 1928.

This is the kind of foundation the builders we cover need. Talent has never been Detroit's problem. Rooms to grow it in have been. A new home for music, with studios and an academy inside, is a city investing in its next generation of artists out loud.

The old hall stood for a century. The new one is being built for the next.
