Detroit Opened a Whole Museum for Its Kids to Make Art

The RED, Detroit's first museum dedicated entirely to children's visual arts, opened in Grandmont Rosedale, founded by artist Yvette Rock.

A Near Future Editorial

July 2026

$3

What a kid pays to spend a day making art at The RED

Detroit opened a museum built entirely for its kids. The RED, which its organizers call the first museum in the city dedicated fully to children's visual arts, opened this spring on a commercial strip of Grand River Avenue in the Grandmont Rosedale neighborhood, tucked between a smoothie shop and a bookstore.

It is not a look-but-do-not-touch kind of place. Inside The RED, kids find artwork made by local youth on the walls, hands-on stations where they make their own pieces, and an open studio to just create. Admission is free for members and runs only a few dollars for everyone else, which is the whole point.

"A kid who sees their own art on a museum wall starts believing they are allowed to make more."

The founder is Detroit artist Yvette Rock, and this is her second run at the idea. She first opened a version in 2018 on Clairmount Avenue to fill a gap she saw for local kids, then rebuilt it as a nonprofit so it could actually grow and last. That patience is the difference between a pop-up and an institution.

Access is the quiet radical part. Most museums teach a kid that art lives behind an admission price and a security rope. The RED puts the brush in their hand, in their own neighborhood, for the price of a snack. That reframes who art is for before a child is old enough to be told otherwise.

This is the earliest version of what ANF covers. Before the founder, before the artist with a following, there is a kid who was handed a space and told to create. Detroit just built that space on purpose.

The next generation of Detroit artists is in that room right now, seeing themselves on the wall.
