He Started a Company at 17. By 21 It Had Raised $17 Million.

Ben Botvinick started Hyper, a payment platform for entrepreneurs, at 17. It raised a $17.2M Series A and helped launch 100-plus businesses.

A Near Future Editorial

September 2026

$17.2M

The Series A Hyper raised, from a company Botvinick started at 17.

Most 17-year-olds are thinking about a summer job. Ben Botvinick was thinking about how hard it is for a normal person to actually start charging money for what they make. In 2020, at 17, he started Hyper, a payment platform built for entrepreneurs rather than established companies.

He bet everything on building it. Botvinick became a Thiel Fellow, the program that pays young people to build instead of finishing school, and put his energy into Hyper full time, turning a teenage idea into real infrastructure for people trying to turn a hustle into a business.

The money followed the product. Hyper raised a 17.2 million dollar Series A from General Catalyst and others, and the platform has helped more than 100 side hustles grow into real businesses generating tens of thousands of dollars in annual revenue apiece.

"At 17 he was not looking for a job. He was building the tool other founders needed."

The instinct that sets Botvinick apart is not that he was young, it is that he built for other builders. Instead of chasing a flashy consumer app, he made the unglamorous plumbing that lets other people get paid, which is often where the most durable companies quietly live.

This is the builder path ANF keeps spotlighting. Build the tool that helps other people build. Botvinick did it at 17 and raised millions doing it.

He started at 17. The rest of the world caught up with a check.
