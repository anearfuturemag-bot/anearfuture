Detroit Just Put 8,000 Young People to Work

Grow Detroit's Young Talent enters its 12th year with more than 13 million dollars raised and 8,000 summer jobs for residents ages 14 to 24. This is what betting on youth looks like.

A Near Future Editorial

July 2026

8,000

Summer jobs for Detroiters ages 14 to 24

Grow Detroit's Young Talent is back for its 12th year, and the numbers are the whole story. More than 13 million dollars raised this cycle. Eight thousand summer jobs for young Detroiters between the ages of 14 and 24. The mayor, city council, and local business leaders launched it together, which tells you who is willing to put real money behind the city's youth.

Twelve years is not a pilot program. It is a commitment. A one summer initiative can be a photo op. A dozen of them in a row is infrastructure, the kind that quietly reshapes what a generation of Detroiters believes is possible for them.

"Betting on young people isn't charity. It's the most direct investment a city can make in its own future."

A first job at 16 does more than pay. It teaches a young person how a workplace runs, how to be counted on, how money moves. For a teenager who has never seen anyone in their family work a professional job, that summer can be the first proof that the door is even open.

The scale is easy to underrate. Eight thousand is not a boutique program. It is a meaningful share of a generation of young Detroiters getting a paycheck, a manager, and a reason to be somewhere with purpose this summer. Programs at that size don't just help individuals. They shift what an entire peer group considers normal.

There is also a retention story hiding inside the jobs number. Young people who build their first professional relationships in Detroit are more likely to imagine their future here. Every summer that keeps a teenager engaged is a small bet against the old story of leaving for opportunity somewhere else.

Here is where the work continues. The city is doing its part by getting thousands of young people in the building. The open question is what happens in September, the platforms, mentors, and pipelines that keep that talent here and building instead of leaving. That is the exact gap A Near Future exists to close.

Detroit is investing early. The rest of us have to make sure there is somewhere for that momentum to go.
