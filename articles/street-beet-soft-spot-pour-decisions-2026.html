Street Beet Just Stacked a Third Concept on One Corktown Corner

It started in 2018 as a vegan pop-up in borrowed kitchens. This week Nina Paletta and Meghan Shaw opened Soft Spot, a dairy-free ice cream window, and Pour Decisions, an oat-milk coffee counter, inside their Corktown restaurant. A lesson in building a footprint one idea at a time.

A Near Future Editorial

August 2026

2018

The year Street Beet started as a pop-up with no permanent kitchen

There is a myth that keeps a lot of young builders parked in place, and it goes like this: you need the whole thing figured out before you can start. The full menu, the permanent space, the perfect launch. Street Beet is the Detroit answer to that myth, and this week it added another chapter to the argument.

The vegan spot in Corktown just opened two new concepts under its own roof. Soft Spot is a walk-up window serving dairy-free ice cream. Pour Decisions is a coffee counter that runs exclusively on oat milk, and it opened August 20. Neither one is a whole new restaurant with a whole new lease. They are extensions of a footprint that Street Beet has been building since it had no footprint at all.

Rewind to 2018. Founders Nina Paletta and Meghan Shaw were not restaurateurs. They were two people cooking vegan food inside whatever room would take them, starting out of Blue Velvet Vintage, a shop in Eastern Market, then moving through brunches, MOCAD, and any venue in the city that opened a door. Their pop-ups had names that told you exactly what they were doing to the fast-food canon: Vegan Taco Hell, McDaddy's, plant-based takes on the chains everybody grew up on.

"Nobody handed them the corner. They stacked it, one idea at a time, out of rooms they did not own."

The turning point was a viral one. A Facebook event for a Vegan Taco Hell pop-up at Corktown's Lager House pulled interest from thousands of people, and Street Beet went from a side project to a name with a real following. That following is what let them do the hard thing most pop-ups never reach, which is convert momentum into a permanent address.

In late 2025 they took over the former Bobcat Bonnie's space on Michigan Avenue in Corktown and turned it into a brick-and-mortar diner, plant-based comfort food and arcade games in a neighborhood that has watched a lot of concepts come and go. That was the second act. Soft Spot and Pour Decisions are the third, built not by signing another lease across town but by looking at the space they already had and asking what else it could hold.

3

Concepts now operating on one Corktown corner: the diner, the coffee counter, the ice cream window

That is the part worth studying if you are trying to build anything in this city. Street Beet did not wait for a business plan that covered a coffee shop and an ice cream window and a full restaurant all at once. They started with the smallest possible version, a pop-up with a folding table and a menu of tacos and an ice cream sandwich, and they added the next thing only when they had earned the room to add it. Eight years later, that patience reads as a coffee counter and a dessert window bolted onto a restaurant they own the vibe of.

There is a Detroit lesson threaded through all of it, too. Both new concepts are built around the same plant-based ethic Street Beet started with, oat milk in the coffee, dairy-free in the ice cream, no pivot away from the thing that made them. Growth did not mean becoming something else to reach more people. It meant giving the people who already showed up two more reasons to stay.

Where to find it

What opened: Soft Spot, a walk-up window for dairy-free ice cream, and Pour Decisions, an oat-milk coffee counter, both inside Street Beet's Corktown restaurant. Pour Decisions opened August 20, 2026.

Where: Street Beet, the former Bobcat Bonnie's space on Michigan Avenue in Corktown, Detroit.

Who built it: Founders Nina Paletta and Meghan Shaw, who have run Street Beet as a Detroit vegan brand since 2018. Follow the latest at @streetbeetdetroit.

If you are sitting on an idea and waiting for the version of it that has everything, Street Beet is the counterargument standing on a Corktown corner. You do not need the finished thing. You need a first thing, an audience that shows up, and the discipline to add the next piece only when you are ready to hold it. The footprint comes later. It always does.
