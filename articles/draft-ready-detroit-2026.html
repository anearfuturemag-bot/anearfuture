Draft Ready Detroit Wants Its Athletes to Own the Game, Not Just Play It

A new initiative launched inside Detroit Public Schools this week, teaching student athletes NIL, financial literacy, and entrepreneurship before a single pro contract arrives. Co-founders Cimone Casson and Dr. Philip Hickman are building the ecosystem the game usually skips.

A Near Future Editorial

August 2026

Every Detroit kid with a jump shot or a forty time has heard the same promise their whole life: the talent will take care of everything. Get the offer, get the ride, get to the league, and the rest works itself out. It is a beautiful story, and it leaves out the part where most of the money, the leverage, and the ownership goes to everyone standing around the athlete instead of the athlete. Draft Ready Detroit was built to close that gap.

This week the initiative launched its pilot program in partnership with Detroit Public Schools Community District. It is not a camp and it is not a combine. It is an athlete development ecosystem, and the whole premise is that a young athlete needs more than exposure. They need a system around them before the offers start coming.

1

Ecosystem connecting schools, industry, technology, and former pros around Detroit student athletes

The pilot serves a select cohort of Detroit student athletes and puts real curriculum in front of them: leadership development, workforce readiness, entrepreneurship education, financial literacy, NIL preparedness, mentorship, and career pathways built on technology and actual industry relationships. In plain terms, it teaches a teenager how their name, image, and likeness translate into a business, and how to hold onto it once it does.

The two people behind it are not outsiders to any of this. Cimone Casson, Co-Founder and Chief Economic Opportunity Officer, is an insurance and risk management strategist whose lane is protecting athletes and building economic power around them. Dr. Philip Hickman, Co-Founder and Chief Innovation Architect, is a nationally recognized education and workforce innovation leader, and the technology platform at the center of Draft Ready runs under his direction.

"Today's athletes need more than exposure. They need an ecosystem."

That line from Casson is the whole thesis. "The sports landscape has changed dramatically," she said at the launch. "NIL opportunities, personal branding, entrepreneurship, technology, and workforce development are now part of an athlete's journey. Draft Ready is building the bridge between athletic talent and lifelong opportunity." Hickman framed the mission in terms of what happens after the last game: "We are preparing athletes for careers, leadership, and economic mobility."

The initiative carries weight beyond its founders. Former NFL veteran Eugene Monroe, a long-time advocate for athlete empowerment, joined the effort, and his message to the cohort was direct. "Student-athletes deserve access to opportunities that extend far beyond the field," Monroe said. "Draft Ready is creating a model that helps athletes understand their value, leverage their platforms, and build meaningful futures for themselves and their communities." The leadership group also includes Jay Alexander, Executive Director of Athletics at DPSCD, alongside strategist Toine Murphy and policy leader Eric Foster.

Read that roster again and the point becomes obvious. It combines athletics, education, workforce development, public policy, and athlete advocacy in one room, aimed at one group of kids. That is not a clinic. That is infrastructure, and infrastructure is the thing Detroit talent has almost never had built specifically for it.

The timing is not an accident either. NIL, personal branding, and the technology reshaping youth sports have turned every promising high schooler into a potential business overnight, whether they were taught how to run one or not. Most are not taught. The kids who figure it out early keep their leverage. The ones who do not sign away pieces of themselves before they understand what a piece is worth. Draft Ready is betting that the difference between those two outcomes is a system, and that a Detroit public school is exactly the right place to build it.

"The goal is not to get drafted. The goal is to own something on the way there and after."

Draft Ready says it wants to become a national model, and the Detroit pilot is the proof of concept. If it works, the version of the story a young athlete hears changes. The talent still opens the door. But it stops being the whole plan.

The ANF Take: the next generation of Detroit athletes will not just get drafted, they will get equity. Building the map before the money arrives is the entire move, and it is the difference between a highlight reel and a legacy. Detroit just decided to teach that in the building, to its own kids, before anyone else does it for them.
