Everyone Your Age Just Started a Business

Business applications from Gen Z jumped 66 percent in a single year, faster than any other generation. AI knocked the cost of starting down to a weekend. The barrier that stopped everyone before you is gone.

A Near Future Editorial

August 2026

66%

Jump in Gen Z business applications, year over year

The paperwork tells the story. New business applications filed by Gen Z jumped 66 percent in June compared to a year earlier, outpacing every older generation, according to figures from the Bank of America Institute reported by CBS News. Across the whole country, Americans filed more than 531,000 business applications in that single month, more than double the 2020 pace.

Read that again. The generation with the least money, the shortest resumes, and the most doubt cast on it is now filing to start companies faster than anyone else. That is not a fluke. It is a shift in who thinks building is even possible.

"The two things that used to stop you, money and time, just got taken off the table."

The reason is not mysterious. Experts point straight at AI. Something that would have taken an app developer three months and 15,000 dollars can now be built over a weekend for close to nothing. The tools that used to require a payroll, a designer, a marketer, a bookkeeper, now sit inside a browser tab. A first-time founder with no budget can operate like a small team.

That is the whole game. For as long as anyone can remember, the biggest thing standing between a young person with an idea and an actual business was capacity. You could only do so much alone, and hiring cost money you did not have. AI quietly deleted that ceiling. The cost of starting fell, and the moment it fell, a generation moved.

There is a harder truth underneath the numbers too. AI is also coming for the entry-level jobs that used to be the safe path, and a lot of young people are choosing to build for themselves precisely because the old ladder is being pulled up. When the safe route stops being safe, betting on your own thing stops looking reckless and starts looking smart.

The surge is not evenly spread, and it is not automatic. A quarter of owners are not touching AI at all, and plenty who do say it adds complexity or overpromises. Filing an application is not the same as building something that lasts. But the direction is unmistakable, and it is pointed at the exact people ANF exists to cover.

So here is the question for anyone reading in Detroit and beyond. The excuse was always money and time. Both are gone. What is left is the decision to start, and the people your age who already made it are not waiting for you to catch up.
